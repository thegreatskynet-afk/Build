06

What moves the needle

Six habits that sports nutrition research keeps coming back to. The recipes handle the food. These handle the rest.

0.7–1 g / lb

Hit protein daily

That is 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg of body weight a day. Go to the top of the range when you are cutting.

25–40 g × 3–5

Spread it out

Split protein over three to five meals instead of one huge dinner. Most recipes here land in that window.

± 10–20%

Keep the change small

A modest deficit protects muscle and a modest surplus limits fat gain. Aim to lose about 0.5 to 1% of body weight a week, or gain about 0.25 to 0.5%.

1–4 h before

Put carbs near training

Eat a carb-heavy meal one to four hours before hard sessions, and carbs plus protein afterward when you train again within a day.

14 g / 1,000 kcal

Eat your fiber

Fiber keeps you full in a deficit. Raise it gradually and keep it lower in the meal right before competition.

7–9 h

Sleep and track the trend

Short sleep blunts recovery and raises appetite. Judge progress by your weekly average weight, not one morning.

Macros are per serving and estimated from standard nutrition values for the listed ingredients. Brands vary, so weigh your food and check labels if you need exact numbers.

Meal prices are rough estimates for the ingredient amounts each recipe uses, whole batch included, at US average grocery prices in late 2026. Chicken, ground beef, eggs, milk and bread follow Bureau of Labor Statistics averages for September 2026, and other items are estimated. Pantry spices are not counted. Your store will differ.

Daily targets use the Mifflin-St Jeor equation and are a starting estimate. Adjust them by how your weight actually trends over two to three weeks.

Profiles, targets and notes are general starting points, not medical or dietetic advice. Weight cuts for combat sports should be planned with a coach or dietitian.
